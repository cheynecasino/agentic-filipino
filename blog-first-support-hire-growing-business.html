Business Growth Guide

What role should you hire first in a growing business?

One of the most expensive mistakes growing businesses make is hiring the wrong support role first. The goal is not just to add headcount. The goal is to remove the bottleneck that is slowing growth, stealing founder time, or creating avoidable inconsistency in the business.

Best fit: founder-led and growing businesses that know they need support, but are not yet sure whether the first hire should be admin, customer support, lead follow-up, operations, or something more specialized.

Start with the bottleneck that keeps repeating

The first hire should usually be attached to the problem that keeps showing up every week. If the founder is always buried in scheduling, inbox work, client follow-up, support tickets, or operational cleanup, that pattern is the clue. The right first hire is often the role that gives the business back time, speed, and consistency.

If the founder is drowning in admin, hire admin support first

For many businesses, the cleanest first hire is an executive assistant or admin support role. This is usually the best move when calendars are messy, inboxes are overloaded, basic coordination is eating up decision-making time, or important small tasks keep slipping.

  • Calendar and inbox management
  • Meeting coordination
  • Follow-up support
  • Document and SOP cleanup

If leads are leaking, hire follow-up support first

If the business is already getting leads but response times are inconsistent, the best first hire may be a lead follow-up assistant or CRM support hire. This is especially true in real estate, local service businesses, and founder-led B2B sales teams. Slow follow-up usually becomes a revenue problem long before it looks like a staffing problem.

If customer questions are piling up, hire customer support first

Ecommerce brands, agencies, and service businesses often need customer support sooner than they realize. If replies are delayed, tickets are stacking up, or the founder is still answering too many customer messages directly, support coverage can create one of the fastest early wins.

If the business feels messy, hire operations support first

Some businesses do not need more front-line help first. They need more structure. If the team is constantly chasing updates, projects are loosely managed, reporting is inconsistent, or processes live only inside the founder's head, an operations coordinator or project support role may create the biggest lift.

Do not hire a "do everything" role if the work is unclear

One common mistake is trying to hire one person to solve five unrelated problems. That usually creates a vague role, unclear priorities, and frustration on both sides. A better move is to define the first support lane clearly, then hire around that lane well.

What we usually recommend first

For many growing businesses, the best first hire is one of these:

  • Executive assistant if founder time is the biggest bottleneck
  • Customer support specialist if service consistency is slipping
  • Lead follow-up assistant if opportunities are being missed
  • Operations coordinator if execution is messy and reactive

The best first hire is the one that gives the business leverage

The right first hire should not just help a little. It should unlock more focus, faster response, cleaner execution, and better follow-through. That is what creates momentum. The businesses that hire well early usually make every future hire easier too.
