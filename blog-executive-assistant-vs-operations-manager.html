Executive assistant vs operations manager: which should you hire first?

This is one of the most important early hiring decisions in a growing business. Both roles create leverage, but they solve very different bottlenecks. The right choice depends on what is actually slowing the business down right now.

Best fit: founders deciding whether they need personal leverage first or broader operational structure first.

Hire an executive assistant if the founder is the bottleneck

If the main problem is an overloaded calendar, inbox, follow-up burden, and too much coordination stuck to the founder, an executive assistant is often the right first hire.

  • Calendar and meeting management
  • Inbox protection
  • Task follow-through
  • Light coordination and admin support

Hire an operations manager if execution is messy across the business

If projects are slipping, processes are unclear, ownership is fuzzy, or the team is reactive all week, operations support may create a bigger lift.

One protects the founder, the other protects the system

That is the cleanest way to think about the difference. An executive assistant gives the founder leverage. An operations manager gives the business structure.

Many businesses need both eventually

The real question is which pain is more expensive today. If the founder is overloaded, start there. If the business is disorganized across multiple people and workflows, operations support may come first.
